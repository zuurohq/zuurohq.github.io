2. What each number means

In plain words, with the exact way each one was worked out.

Net profit

Total closed profit minus losses and costs in the period, after the $600 daily limit.

Growth on $1,000

Net profit divided by your original $1,000. It looks big because no new money is added and size stays fixed.

Growth on start balance

Net profit divided by what the account held when the period began. This is the fairer measure: fixed size earns similar dollars on a bigger balance, so the % shrinks.

Profit factor (PF)

Total money won on winning trades divided by total money lost on losing trades. Above 1 = profit. 1.30 means $1.30 won for every $1 lost.

Max drawdown

The biggest fall from the highest balance reached (the high-water mark) to a later low, using each day's closing result. Not a sum of all losses. Intraday dips are not counted, so the real figure can be larger.

Max drawdown %

The fall as a share of the peak balance. 100% means the account hit zero.

Sharpe ratio

Average daily profit divided by how much daily results swing, scaled to a year. Above 1 is decent, above 2 is strong. It is high in the last 90 days because results were smooth and positive.

Maximum loss hit

The $600 daily limit was reached: trading stops for the day. Assumes the stop fills exactly at -$600; a fast market could cost a little more.

8. What this says in plain words

  • Over the last year the three strategies made about $7,094 on 3 micros (+709% of $1,000), with a profit factor of only 1.30.
  • The worst fall was $2,562 over 99 trading days, from $2,394 (20 Nov 2025) to -$168 (8 Apr 2026). It was many losing days, with five days of -$400 to -$600 doing most of the damage, not one bad day.
  • Starting exactly a year ago with $1,000, the balance would have touched $500 on 12 Jan 2026 and gone below zero on 8 Apr 2026, before the strong summer. Under rule 4 you would have stopped at $500 and never seen the recovery.
  • The $600 limit was last hit on 12 Jun 2026. Since then the worst day was -$414.
  • The last 90 days were much better ($4,070, +101% on the balance at the start of that stretch, PF 1.47); December to March were the losing months. Recent strength is not the new normal.
  • The middle projection for a fixed 3 micros ends the year near $4,815, but 46% of simulated years hit the $500 stop on the way, and 57% if the edge is 30% weaker.
  • Everything here is a replay of past data, with the strategies tuned on the same period. Treat the numbers as a ceiling, not a promise.
