2. What each number means

In plain words, with the exact way each one was worked out.

Net profit

Total closed profit minus losses and costs in the period, after the $600 daily limit.

Growth on $1,000

Net profit divided by your original $1,000. It looks big because no new money is added and size stays fixed.

Growth on start balance

Net profit divided by what the account held when the period began. This is the fairer measure: fixed size earns similar dollars on a bigger balance, so the % shrinks.

Profit factor (PF)

Total money won on winning trades divided by total money lost on losing trades. Above 1 = profit. 1.30 means $1.30 won for every $1 lost.

Max drawdown

The biggest fall from the highest balance reached (the high-water mark) to a later low, using each day's closing result. Not a sum of all losses. Intraday dips are not counted, so the real figure can be larger.

Max drawdown %

The fall as a share of the peak balance. 100% means the account hit zero.

Sharpe ratio

Average daily profit divided by how much daily results swing, scaled to a year. Above 1 is decent, above 2 is strong. It is high in the last 90 days because results were smooth and positive.

Worst day

With no daily limit, nothing stops a bad day. This is the largest one-day loss in the period. Days of -$600 or worse are marked, because a $600 limit would have stopped them.

8. What this says in plain words

  • Over the last year the three strategies made about $9,620 on 3 micros (+962% of $1,000), with a profit factor of only 1.28.
  • The worst fall was $2,004 over 74 trading days, from $2,419 (20 Nov 2025) to $415 (4 Mar 2026). It was many losing days, with a handful of big losing days doing most of the damage, not one bad day.
  • Starting exactly a year ago with $1,000, the balance would have touched $500 on 12 Jan 2026 and bottomed at $415 on 4 Mar 2026, before the strong summer. Under rule 4 you would have stopped at $500 and never seen the recovery.
  • The last day to lose $600 or more was 4 Mar 2026. Since then the worst day was -$363. With no limit, a single bad day can take most of a $1,000 account (the worst in 3 years was -$970 on 10 Apr 2025).
  • The last 90 days were much better ($3,660, +53% on the balance at the start of that stretch, PF 1.42); December to March were the losing months. Recent strength is not the new normal.
  • The middle projection for a fixed 3 micros ends the year near $8,566, but 35% of simulated years hit the $500 stop on the way, and 49% if the edge is 30% weaker.
  • Everything here is a replay of past data, with the strategies tuned on the same period. Treat the numbers as a ceiling, not a promise.
