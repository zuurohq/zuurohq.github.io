Read this first

  • What this is: a replay of past data. The strategies were tuned on this same period, so real trading will probably do worse than anything shown here.
  • 3 micros is a big position for $1,000. Every 100-point move in the Nasdaq-100 is $600, which is 60% of the account. One day lost $695 (4 Mar 2026). The worst day in 3 years lost $786. Counting trades still open (with the stops honoured), the balance fell to about $110 on 5 Mar 2026, the session after the $415 close.
  • There is no stop rule and no daily limit, and this page models none. Every number here assumes you keep trading at 3 micros whatever happens, down to the point the account cannot hold 3 micros ($300). Nothing stops you trading down. A year-ago start first closed at or below $600 on 17 Dec 2025 ($566) and bottomed at $415 (4 Mar 2026), a 58% fall, before the strong spring and summer.
  • Chance the balance falls to $600 or less at a daily close within the next year ($600 is a reference point, not a rule): about 2 in 5 (42%) if the next year looks like the last three; about 3 in 5 (58%) if the strategies are 30% weaker; about 7 in 10 (72%) if 60% weaker. Most of that risk comes early, while the cushion is thin: about 1 in 5 simulated accounts touch $600 within 10 trading days (20%) and 3 in 10 within the first month (30%). About 1 in 4 simulated years (25%) end below $300, too little to hold 3 micros. The profit rests on very few trades: the best 28 of 2,789 trades (1%) make 91% of the 3-year profit. If the strategies have no edge at all, 80% of simulated years end below $300 and the middle outcome is $255. Losses can be large and quick: in about 1 in 8 simulated first months the balance falls $700 or more below its start at some daily close, and about 1 in 250 simulated years end at or below $0.
  • Tradovate can close your positions during the day, with or without warning. It requires $100 of margin for each open micro at all times, and its policy says it may liquidate an account with or without prior notification. A real Tradovate email ("margin requirements are close to auto-liquidation") reached you when net liquidation value was about 1.9 times day margin ($934 against $500 with 5 micros open); that email reports the situation and should not be relied on as an early warning. Tradovate does not publish the exact line. On last year's path, counting the micros actually open, the balance fell below twice the margin on 4 days, the first on 13 Jan 2026. None of the results on this page include these close-outs or their $25/$50 fees.
  • Futures losses are not capped at your deposit. A fast market can take more than you put in. Only use money you can afford to lose in full. You have chosen to forward-test live with $1,000 instead of paper testing, so the first weeks of real trading are the test. Read section 9 before the first trade.

Your rules

  1. Trade a fixed 3 micros. Do not add contracts as the balance grows.
  2. Per-trade stops: GEN005 $300 and NAVI $150 per contract (built into the Personal copies). ST13 has its own stops and a permanent stop that in practice triggers at about a $480 loss (its $800 limit minus its own safety reserve).
  3. No daily loss limit (your choice). A single bad day is not capped.
  4. The three strategies are separate scripts and cannot see each other: if one signals against a position another holds, skip that signal and log it (it happened 32 times in 3 years).
  5. Forward test live with $1,000 (your decision; no paper test). Treat the first weeks as the test and log every day. You have chosen not to set any stop rule; this page therefore shows the full downside of trading on.

  • The worst stretch ran 74 trading days: from $2,419 on 20 Nov 2025 to $415 at the close on 4 Mar 2026, after a single day lost $695. Counting trades still open (with the stops honoured), the balance fell to about $110 on 5 Mar 2026, the session after that close. That is the account, essentially gone, for a moment.

It then recovered, but with $600 crossed more than once, someone trading this live would have been under real pressure before the recovery.
  • The last day to lose $600 or more was 4 Mar 2026. Since then the worst day was -$424 (3 Jun 2026). With no daily loss limit nothing stops a bad day; the worst in 3 years was -$786 (14 Apr 2025).

If the strategies are 30% weaker, 58% touch $600 and 40% end below $300; if 60% weaker, 72% and 58%, and the middle outcome is only $285.

9. What to do next

Before the first trade

  1. Signals reach Tradovate through MetaCopier (your choice). Send MetaCopier the message sheet (METACOPIER_TRADE_LIFECYCLE_PAYLOADS) and wait for its written answers. No alerts are created until you say so.
  2. Test in order: a dry run (messages arrive, no orders), then a demo account, then 1 micro live. Move to the next step only when the log matches TradingView.
  3. Decide what happens when strategies disagree. About once a month one strategy opens against another's position (32 times in 3 years). Netting is handled by MetaCopier; check on the demo that a flatten by one strategy does not close another's position.
  4. Set the alerts at 1 micro per strategy on the two Personal layouts, following your live-test step sheet (LIVE_FORWARD_TEST_STEPS_FOR_OWNER).
  5. Know what Tradovate can do to you. Its Risk Settings (Daily Loss Limit, Weekly Loss Limit, Trailing Max Drawdown) close all positions and lock trading for the rest of that period. Once locked, Tradovate staff cannot unlock it early. These are hard stops, not alerts. Whether you set any is your decision; this page assumes none. Note: GEN005's per-trade stop and 4:40 pm flatten messages are being added to the MetaCopier route; until confirmed, those exits may not reach Tradovate.

While trading

  1. Every day: log the date, entry and exit times, your fill, TradingView's price for the same fill, and the day's profit. Check the account shows the same position as the strategies.
  2. Look into it the same day if: an alert is missed, a fill is more than 2 ticks from TradingView's, the account holds a position no strategy shows, or Tradovate closes a position. Do not add money just to keep trading.
  3. Review after week 1 and week 2: fills against the backtest, slippage and missed trades. ST13 only starts on 18 Oct 2026. Day margin applies from the 6 pm open, so GEN005's 6 pm entries are covered.

Margin, costs and forced close-outs (Tradovate's own pages, 7 Oct 2026; confirm in your account)

  • Day margin for MNQ is $100 per contract ($300 for 3 micros); it fits inside $1,000. Initial (overnight) margin is $4,796.79 per contract, far above your balance, so any position still open 15 minutes before the 5 pm New York close is closed by Tradovate's desk. NAVI flattens at 4:30 pm and GEN005 at 4:40 pm, safely before that cut-off (tested: GEN005 3-year profit $8,245 at 4:40 pm vs $8,204 at 4:45 pm, same 1,549 trades).
  • Tradovate monitors positions in real time and may liquidate positions with or without prior notification; an email can arrive after the event (a real one read: margin requirements are close to auto-liquidation, net liquidation $934 against a $500 day margin). If the account does not meet margin at all times, the position may be liquidated, with a $25 fee the first time and $50 after.
  • 15 minutes before major economic news, intraday margin is 4 times normal for new entries (about $400 per micro, $1,200 for 3). On a $1,000 account, entries near those releases (for example 8:30 am data) can be rejected, so some backtest trades will not happen.
  • Costs per side: $0.39 commission on the free plan ($0.29 monthly, $0.09 lifetime) plus exchange $0.35, NFA $0.01 and clearing $0.19, so $0.94 per side. The simulations assume $0.95, so costs match. Your balance must also cover each trade's commission and fees.

Sources: Tradovate pricing; Tradovate MNQ margin and fees; Tradovate margin table; Tradovate margin policy article.

B. What each term means

Micro (MNQ)
A micro Nasdaq-100 futures contract. $2 per index point per micro, so 3 micros = $6 a point.
Net profit
Closed profit minus losses and costs, with no daily loss limit and with the per-trade stops.
Growth on $1,000
Net profit divided by your $1,000. It is large because 3 micros is a large position for $1,000. The same size makes losses just as large.
Growth on start balance
Net profit divided by what the account held when the period began, if you had started a year ago. It shrinks as the balance grows.
Profit factor (PF)
Money won on winning trades divided by money lost on losing trades. 1.28 means $1.28 won for each $1 lost.
Max drawdown
The biggest fall from the highest balance (the high-water mark) to a later low, using each day's closing result. Not a sum of losses. Intraday dips are not counted, so the real figure can be larger.
Sharpe ratio
Average daily profit divided by how much daily results swing, scaled to a year. Back-tests of tuned strategies usually show a much higher Sharpe than live trading; this same year also had a 83% fall from its peak.
Win rate
Share of trades that made money. Most trades here lose; a few big winners carry the profit.
Net liquidation value
What your Tradovate account would be worth if everything were closed now: cash plus the profit or loss on open trades. It is shown on your Tradovate screen.
$600 reference line
A level used on this page to show how often the account gets close to the margin needed for 3 micros ($300 plus a buffer). It is not a rule and nothing acts on it.
Poor / middle / good (projection)
1 in 10 simulated years end below "poor"; half end above "middle"; 1 in 10 end above "good".
