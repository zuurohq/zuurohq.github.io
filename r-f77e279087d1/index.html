Read this first

  • This is a replay of past data on which the strategies were tuned (and the choice of GEN005's E12 engine was made on the same data). Real trading will probably do worse. The profit rests on very few trades: the best 20 of 2,006 (1%) make 69% of the 3-year profit.
  • 3 micros is a big position for $1,000. A 100-point Nasdaq-100 move is $600, 60% of the account. One day lost $665 (4 Mar 2026); counting trades still open, the balance fell to about $298 the session after.
  • There is no stop rule and no daily limit, and this page models none. Every figure assumes you keep trading 3 micros whatever happens. If the next year looks like the last three (simulated, 20,000 runs): 37% of years fall to $600 or less at a daily close (a reference point, not a rule), 20% end below $300, and in about 1 in 10 first months the balance falls $700 or more. 27% touch $600 within the first month.
  • The worst cases are worse than the charts show. The charts freeze an account at $300, but nothing in your plan stops you there. If you keep trading, 10% of simulated years (about 1 in 10) reach $0 if the past repeats, 22% if the strategies are 30% weaker, 41% if 60% weaker, and 72% if they have no edge at all (a middle outcome of $0).
  • Measured against the project's own promotion gates (profit factor, drawdown, top-1% removal, cost stress) this plan fails, and every independent review has rated it FAIL for money decisions. The live test is your decision and your risk.
  • Tradovate can close your positions during the day, with or without warning (its policy says so). It needs $100 of margin per open micro at all times. A real Tradovate email ("margin requirements are close to auto-liquidation") reached you at net liquidation $934 against a $500 day margin; it reports the situation and is not an early warning. None of the results here include close-outs or their $25/$50 fees. On last year's path there was no day when the balance, counting open trades, fell below twice the margin.
  • Futures losses are not capped at your deposit. Use only money you can lose in full. You chose to forward-test live instead of paper testing, so the first weeks are the test. Read section 9 before the first trade.

Your rules

  1. Trade a fixed 3 micros: ST13 1, NAVI 1, GEN005 1. GEN005 runs its E12 engine only (its B11 engine is switched off), so at most 3 micros are ever open. Do not add contracts as the balance grows.
  2. Per-trade stops: GEN005 $300 and NAVI $150 per contract (built into the Personal copies). These stops exist in TradingView and reach Tradovate only as messages through MetaCopier: GEN005's entry message carries no stop level, and GEN005 can hold overnight until its 4:40 pm flatten, so one lost message means no stop for that trade. ST13 has its own stops and a permanent stop that in practice triggers at about a $480 loss (its $800 limit minus its own safety reserve).
  3. No daily loss limit (your choice). A single bad day is not capped.
  4. The three strategies are separate scripts and cannot see each other. One signalled against another's open position 31 times in 3 years. MetaCopier has answered in writing (7 Oct 2026): Tradovate nets all MNQ contracts per account, and MetaCopier does not separate the strategies' signals before sending orders, so this must be tested on the demo before going live.
  5. Forward test live with $1,000 (your decision; no paper test). Treat the first weeks as the test and log every day. You have chosen not to set any stop rule; the worst cases on this page assume you keep trading whatever happens.

9. What to do next

Before the first trade

  1. Signals reach Tradovate through MetaCopier (your choice). Send MetaCopier the message package (METACOPIER_GEN005_UPDATE for GEN005; METACOPIER_QUESTIONS_FOR_TRANSLATOR_TEAM and METACOPIER_PAYLOAD_EXAMPLES for NAVI and ST13) and wait for its written answers. No alerts are created until you say so.
  2. Test in order, and move up only when you say go: (1) dry run: alerts created with MetaCopier's address, messages arrive, no orders; (2) demo account: check that a stop-out and the 4:40 pm flatten close the matching Tradovate position; (3) one live micro on one strategy, one trade, to prove the loop; (4) the full plan, 3 strategies at 1 micro each (3 micros, the size every figure here assumes).
  3. Decide what happens when strategies disagree. About once a month one strategy opens against another's position (31 times in 3 years). MetaCopier's written answer is that the broker nets per account and ticker and that strategy signals are not separated. Test on the demo that a flatten or stop by one strategy does not close another's position.
  4. Set the alerts at 1 micro per strategy on the two Personal layouts, following your live-test step sheet (LIVE_FORWARD_TEST_STEPS_FOR_OWNER). Several script defaults are not the plan's values (for example, GEN005's B11 engine defaults to ON; you want it OFF); the step sheet lists what to set.
  5. Know what Tradovate can do to you. Its Risk Settings (Daily Loss Limit, Weekly Loss Limit, Trailing Max Drawdown) close all positions and lock trading: the Daily and Weekly limits until the next period, and Trailing Max Drawdown until you contact support. These are hard stops, not alerts. Whether you set any is your decision; this page assumes none. GEN005's per-trade stop and 4:40 pm flatten now send their own messages; confirm in the dry run and the demo that they close the matching Tradovate position.

While trading

  1. Every day: log the date, entry and exit times, your fill, TradingView's price for the same fill, and the day's profit. Check the account shows the same position as the strategies.
  2. Look into it the same day if: an alert is missed, a fill is more than 2 ticks from TradingView's, the account holds a position no strategy shows, or Tradovate closes a position. Do not add money just to keep trading.
  3. Review after week 1 and week 2: fills against the backtest, slippage and missed trades. ST13 only starts on 18 Oct 2026. Day margin applies from the 6 pm open, so GEN005's 6 pm entries are covered.

Margin, costs and forced close-outs (Tradovate's own pages, 7 Oct 2026; confirm in your account)

  • Day margin for MNQ is $100 per contract ($300 for 3 micros); it fits inside $1,000. Initial (overnight) margin is $4,796.79 per contract, far above your balance, so any position still open 15 minutes before the 5 pm New York close is closed by Tradovate's desk. NAVI flattens at 4:30 pm and GEN005 at 4:40 pm, safely before that cut-off (tested: GEN005 E12-only 3-year profit $8,471 with the 4:40 pm flatten).
  • Tradovate monitors positions in real time and may liquidate positions with or without prior notification; an email can arrive after the event (a real one read: margin requirements are close to auto-liquidation, net liquidation $934 against a $500 day margin). If the account does not meet margin at all times, the position may be liquidated, with a $25 fee the first time and $50 after.
  • 15 minutes before major economic news, intraday margin is 4 times normal for new entries (about $400 per micro, $1,200 for 3). On a $1,000 account, entries near those releases (for example 8:30 am data) can be rejected, so some backtest trades will not happen.
  • Costs per side: $0.39 commission on the free plan ($0.29 monthly, $0.09 lifetime) plus exchange $0.35, NFA $0.01 and clearing $0.19, so $0.94 per side. The simulations assume $0.95, so costs match. Your balance must also cover each trade's commission and fees.

Sources: Tradovate pricing; Tradovate MNQ margin and fees; Tradovate margin table; Tradovate margin policy article.

B. What each term means

Micro (MNQ)
A micro Nasdaq-100 futures contract. $2 per index point per micro, so 3 micros = $6 a point.
Net profit
Closed profit minus losses and costs, with no daily loss limit and with the per-trade stops.
Growth on $1,000
Net profit divided by your $1,000. It is large because 3 micros is a large position for $1,000. The same size makes losses just as large.
Growth on start balance
Net profit divided by what the account held when the period began, if you had started a year ago. It shrinks as the balance grows.
Profit factor (PF)
Money won on winning trades divided by money lost on losing trades. 1.28 means $1.28 won for each $1 lost.
Max drawdown
The biggest fall from the highest balance (the high-water mark) to a later low, using each day's closing result. Not a sum of losses. Intraday dips are not counted, so the real figure can be larger.
Sharpe ratio
Average daily profit divided by how much daily results swing, scaled to a year. Back-tests of tuned strategies usually show a much higher Sharpe than live trading; this same year also had a 77% fall from its peak.
Win rate
Share of trades that made money. Most trades here lose; a few big winners carry the profit.
Net liquidation value
What your Tradovate account would be worth if everything were closed now: cash plus the profit or loss on open trades. It is shown on your Tradovate screen.
$600 reference line
A level used on this page to show how often the account gets close to the margin needed for 3 micros ($300 plus a buffer). It is not a rule and nothing acts on it.
Poor / middle / good (projection)
1 in 10 simulated years end below "poor"; half end above "middle"; 1 in 10 end above "good".
