What if the calls have NO real edge?

We tested the same plan with random buy/sell directions. The estimate falls to about +$131 per try (pass 24%, first payout 5.6%). Part of the profit comes from how the firm's payout rules are built, and the calls roughly double it. If the firm changes its rules or the calls stop working, this can drop to zero or below.

What we tested (and why you can trust it - a little)

We took about 6,000 real past calls (October 2023 to October 2026) and replayed each one on minute-by-minute prices, with realistic costs. For every setup we also ran a "coin-flip" version that picks buy or sell at random. The difference shows how much the calls really help.

Points = extra trades out of 100 that reach the profit level before the stop. We focus on 2024 to today (the recent market). The help is real but small and uneven, which is why this stays a practice plan first.

What can go wrong

The edge fades. Part of 2024 showed no help from the calls. It may do so again.
It has never been traded live. All results are replays and computer estimates of Lucid's rules as published on their help pages.
Lots of tries fail. About 7 in 10 evaluations fail. Plan your budget before you start.
The vendor's own numbers are not audited. Their 80% win rate does not mean 80% profit, we measured the real thing ourselves.
