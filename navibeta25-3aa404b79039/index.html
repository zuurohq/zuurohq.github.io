What to be careful about
  • The latest year looks better than the two before it, but the gap may be luck. A back-test does not promise it continues.
  • Months swing a lot and hold few Evals each. Evals fail in clusters, so buying many at once adds less safety than it looks.

  • Fills use back-test prices. Real slippage can be worse.
How these numbers are counted
  • One Eval = one attempt, from start to pass or fail.
  • Only finished attempts count. An open one is left out.
  • A window counts attempts that started inside it.
  • Costs are included, with 1 tick of slippage. The edge is thin: about $18 more slippage per trade would erase it.
