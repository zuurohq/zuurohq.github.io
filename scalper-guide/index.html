Before you start

Three small checks make the results easier to understand.

01

Keep the chart simple

Use regular candles. Keep one market and timeframe while comparing results.

02

Add real costs

Commission applies when you enter and exit. Slippage is measured in ticks.

03

Change one thing at a time

Run a strategy backtest

Follow the steps in order. Your checkmarks stay in this browser.

01

Open the strategy on your chart

Choose a regular candlestick chart, then pick the market and timeframe you want to study. Add ZQOS Scalper Strategy from your saved scripts or Pine Editor.

02

Set realistic costs

Open the strategy’s Settings → Properties. For the current NQ 15-minute research setup, use these values:

Commission$0.95 / contract / side
Slippage2 ticks / fill
Starting capital$100,000
Order size1 contract
Test margin10% long and short
03

Choose the dates

Open Strategy Tester below the chart. Start with a short check, then repeat with 30, 90, and 365 days. If your plan offers Deep Backtesting, use it for longer history.

If TradingView marks the report as out of date, click Update report.

04

Read the report, not just one number

Check net profit, number of trades, profit factor, and largest drawdown. Open the trade list and look at losing trades too.

Repeat the same settings over other dates before deciding what to keep.

For this research setup: costs are $0.95 per contract on each side and 2 ticks of slippage per fill. The 10% long and short margin is a TradingView test assumption, not a broker requirement. Use assumptions that match your own market and account when you compare results.

Use the indicator

The indicator shows signals and reference levels on the chart.

01

Add the companion indicator

Choose ZQOS Scalper Companion from your saved scripts or Pine Editor. It can share the chart with the strategy.

02

Choose what to display

Open the indicator’s Settings → Inputs. Pick one entry engine and adjust the display groups you want to see.

Signals are confirmed at bar close. The entry mark is not a promise of a fill.

03

Read the chart labels

The dashboard gives the score, direction, and reference status. Lines show the indicator’s reference entry, stop, and targets when those displays are on.

04

Alerts are optional

The indicator provides alert conditions, but it does not create or turn on alerts. If you choose to use them, select a condition in TradingView’s alert dialog and review it before saving.

Quick answers

Short answers to the things people usually ask first.

What are the best settings?

No setting has passed the current NQ 15-minute test yet. Results depend on market, timeframe, dates, account size, and costs. Change one setting at a time; a strong report alone does not prove a setting is best.

Why enter fees and slippage?

Without them, simulated results can look better than they would after costs. Commission applies to both entry and exit.

Why did a signal move before the bar closed?

Some chart previews can change while a bar is forming. Use confirmed bar-close signals for the committed rules.

Does this place trades or make alerts?

No. The strategy runs simulated orders in TradingView. The indicator does not create alerts. Neither connects to a broker.
